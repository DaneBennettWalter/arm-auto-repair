Check Engine Light On? Here's What You Need to Know

Published April 18, 2025 | Engine Diagnostics, Auto Repair

That glowing check engine light can mean anything from a loose gas cap to serious engine problems. Don't ignore it, and don't panic. Professional diagnosis finds the real problem.

Why Is My Check Engine Light On?

Your vehicle's computer (ECM/PCM) monitors hundreds of sensors and systems. When something is out of normal range, it stores a diagnostic trouble code (DTC) and turns on the check engine light.

Common Causes:

  • Oxygen sensor - monitors exhaust emissions
  • Catalytic converter - emissions system failure
  • Mass airflow sensor - measures incoming air
  • Spark plugs or ignition coils - misfiring engine
  • EVAP system leak - fuel vapor system
  • Loose or damaged gas cap - easiest fix!
  • Thermostat - engine temperature issues
  • EGR valve - exhaust gas recirculation

Should I Keep Driving With the Light On?

It depends:

Solid Check Engine Light

Usually safe to drive short distances, but get it diagnosed soon. Continuing to drive with a problem can cause more damage and higher repair costs.

Flashing Check Engine Light

This is serious. A flashing light means active engine misfire that can damage your catalytic converter. Pull over safely and have the vehicle towed or driven very carefully to a repair shop immediately.

Why Not Just Clear the Code?

Some shops or parts stores will scan your codes for free and clear them. But that doesn't fix the problem - it just turns off the light temporarily.

The light will come back if the problem persists. Meanwhile, you might be:

  • Failing emissions testing
  • Getting worse fuel economy
  • Causing more damage
  • Missing other problems (code readers only show active codes)

Proper diagnosis finds and fixes the real problem.

Prevent Check Engine Light Problems

Simple maintenance prevents many check engine light issues:

  • Keep up with oil changes
  • Replace spark plugs on schedule
  • Use quality fuel from reputable stations
  • Tighten your gas cap until it clicks (3 clicks)
  • Address small problems before they become big problems
