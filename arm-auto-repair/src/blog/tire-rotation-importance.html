Tire Rotation: Simple Maintenance That Saves You Money

Published June 5, 2025 | Tire Service, Maintenance

Tire rotation is one of the simplest and most cost-effective maintenance services you can do. It extends tire life by thousands of miles and keeps your vehicle handling safely.

Why Rotate Tires?

Tires don't wear evenly. Front tires (especially on front-wheel-drive vehicles) wear faster than rear tires. Rotating tires distributes wear evenly across all four tires, so they all last longer.

Benefits of Regular Tire Rotation:

  • Longer tire life - get thousands more miles
  • Better handling - even wear means balanced performance
  • Smoother ride - prevents vibrations from uneven wear
  • Improved traction - especially important in rain
  • Warranty compliance - many tire warranties require rotation

How Often Should You Rotate Tires?

Every 5,000-7,500 miles or with every other oil change. Check your owner's manual for your vehicle's recommendation.

If you get oil changes every 5,000 miles, just rotate your tires at the same time - easy to remember.

Tire Rotation Patterns

The correct rotation pattern depends on your vehicle:

  • Front-wheel drive: Front to rear, cross rear to front
  • Rear-wheel drive: Rear to front, cross front to rear
  • All-wheel drive: Cross pattern or X-pattern
  • Directional tires: Front to rear same side only
  • Staggered sizes: Side-to-side only (or can't rotate)

We use the correct pattern for your vehicle and tire type.

What Else We Check During Tire Rotation

When rotating your tires, we also:

  • Inspect tread depth (check for premature wear)
  • Check tire pressure (adjust to proper PSI)
  • Look for damage (cuts, punctures, bulges)
  • Examine brake condition (we can see brakes with wheels off)
  • Inspect suspension components

This catches problems early before they become expensive repairs.

Signs of Uneven Tire Wear

If you see these patterns, bring your vehicle in:

  • Edge wear: Under-inflation or over-inflation
  • Center wear: Over-inflation
  • Cupping/scalloping: Suspension or alignment problem
  • One-sided wear: Alignment issue
  • Feathering: Alignment problem

Uneven wear means something else is wrong. We'll diagnose and fix the underlying problem.
